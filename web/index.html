Six ways to take a picture of a screen

Each one has a shortcut, so the app stays out of your way. Press the keys, drag if you need to, and the editor opens on what you captured.

  • ⇧⌘2

    Capture Area

    Drag a rectangle anywhere on screen. The rest of the display dims so you can see the edges of what you are keeping.

  • ⇧⌘1

    Capture Screen

    A whole display, at its full resolution. Pick the display if you have several.

  • ⇧⌘3

    Capture Window

    Point at a window and take just that window, cleanly separated from whatever is behind it.

  • ⇧⌘4

    Scrolling Capture

    A long page in one image. Scroll while it captures, and the pieces are stitched into a single screenshot.

  • ⇧⌘6

    Capture Text

    Select an area and get the text inside it on your clipboard. Reads directly from the screen — an error dialog, a scanned page, a video still.

  • ⇧⌘5

    Screen Recording

    Record a display or a region to MP4. Requires ffmpeg, which the app finds on its own if you already have it.

Then do something with it

The annotation editor opens on every capture. Draw on it, blur what should not be in it, and save it — or throw it away without it ever reaching your library.

  • Annotate

    Arrows, boxes, highlighter, freehand, text and numbered steps. An untouched capture is saved byte for byte rather than re-encoded.

  • Optimize

    Shrink one image or a folder of them. PNG, JPEG and WebP, with a size limit if you need to hit one.

  • Library

    Everything you kept, on your own disk, in a folder you choose. Nothing is uploaded unless you ask for it.

Share a link when you need one

Cloud upload is the paid part. Press upload on a capture and you get a URL to paste into a message — no attachment, no resizing, no "can you send that again". Plans are priced by how much storage you want, from a few dollars a year.

Anything you upload is reachable by anyone holding the link. Files stay for as long as the plan is active, and for 30 days after it ends before they are deleted. Captures on your own computer are never touched.

Questions people actually ask

Do I have to pay?

Not to capture anything. Every screenshot mode, the annotation editor, text recognition, screen recording and the image optimizer work with no account and no network connection. Paying adds one thing: uploading a capture and getting a link to it.

Why does macOS ask for Screen Recording permission?

Because taking a picture of the screen is exactly what that permission controls — macOS makes no distinction between a screenshot and a recording. Grant it in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording, then quit and reopen Capture Studio. The relaunch matters: macOS answers the permission question once per app launch and caches it.

I granted it, but my captures come back showing only the wallpaper.

That is a stale permission entry, and it happens after an update: the toggle still reads as on while the system treats it as off. Turning it off and on again does not clear it. Select Capture Studio in the Screen Recording list, remove it with the − button, then reopen the app and grant the permission again when it asks.

Do I need ffmpeg?

Only for screen recording. Screenshots, annotation, text recognition and optimizing do not touch it. If you already have ffmpeg the app finds it on its own; if not, brew install ffmpeg is the shortest route.

Where do my captures go?

Into ~/Pictures/CaptureStudio, as ordinary PNG and MP4 files you can open with anything. Settings shows you the folder and opens it in Finder. There is no proprietary library format to be locked into — deleting the app leaves your captures exactly where they were.

Does the app send anything anywhere?

Only when you press upload. There is no analytics, no telemetry and no licence check that phones home — outside of the cloud features the app makes no network requests at all. Text recognition runs on your Mac, not on a server.

What happens to my uploads if I stop paying?

Existing links keep working for 30 days after the plan ends, so nothing you have already shared breaks the moment a card expires. After that the uploaded copies are deleted. The originals on your own disk are never touched.

Which languages can it read?

Text recognition uses the engine built into macOS, so the list depends on your macOS version rather than on us. Capture Studio shows you the ones your system actually supports and lets you pick which to use in Settings.
